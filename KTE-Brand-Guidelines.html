The mark

The identity is a single engraved crest: two lions rampant supporting an oval cartouche that carries the shul's name. It reads as carved brass on parchment, and it should always be reproduced that way — full tonal gold, never flattened into a silhouette.

The cartoucheThe oval and its frame. The shul's name sits here. This is the piece that survives at small sizes.

The crestLions, cartouche and scrollwork together. Use where the dedication lines would be unreadable.

The full lockupCrest plus the two dedication lines and the address. This is the default.

Clear space & minimum size

Clear space

Measure the height of the word קהל inside the oval. Call that X. Keep at least 1X of empty space on all four sides of the logo — roughly one eighth of the logo's width. No text, rule, photo edge, or page trim enters that zone.

The hatched band is the exclusion zone. On a crowded flyer, give it more.

What not to do

The crest is a piece of engraving. Every one of these treatments makes it look like clip art.

Don't stretch itScale proportionally, always. Hold shift.

Don't recolor itThe gold is the identity. No blue, no silver, no team colors.

Don't add effectsNo drop shadows, glows, bevels, or strokes. It is already three-dimensional.

Don't tilt itThe crest is symmetrical and sits level. Always.

Don't place it on goldOr on any mid-tone that swallows the metal. Use ivory, parchment, white, or deep bronze.

Don't wash it outNo screening back to 20%, no low-opacity watermarks behind body text.

  • Don't rebuild the lockup — never re-typeset the Hebrew lines yourself or change their spacing.
  • Don't crop the lions out to make it fit a square. Use the cartouche mark instead.
  • Don't box it in a rectangle, circle, or badge that isn't in this document.
  • Don't set the shul's name in a different typeface next to the crest.

Combinations that work

  • Bronze Shadow text on Ivory — the default for every document.
  • Champagne text on Bronze Shadow — for reverse panels and dark signage.
  • Tiferes Gold rules on Ivory or Highlight Cream — dividers, letterhead trim.
  • Tiferes Gold as body text on ivory. It fails contrast; keep gold for rules and ornament.
  • Champagne on Parchment. Not enough separation to read.

Typography

Three families, each with one job. All three are free, cover Hebrew and Latin between them, and can be installed on any machine or loaded on a website.

Frank Ruhl Libre — headings and all Hebrew

Divrei Torah & שיעורים

A traditional Hebrew serif with a full Latin companion — the closest freely available relative of the letterforms cut into the cartouche. Use Black (900) for titles, Bold (700) for subheads, Regular (400) for Hebrew running text.

Cinzel — the English name and inscriptional lines

KEHAL TIFERES ELIEZER

Roman capitals, matching the address line already cut into the crest. Use it only for the shul's English name, addresses, and dedication plaques — never for a sentence, and never below 8 pt. Track it out to 0.12–0.20 em.

Assistant — body text, forms, signage, screens

A clean bilingual sans that handles English and עברית in one family. Use it for everything functional: paragraphs, tables, schedules, zmanim boards, forms, buttons, and captions. Regular for text, SemiBold for emphasis, Bold for labels.

Hebrew & honorifics

These are not styling preferences. Getting them wrong is the kind of mistake people notice and remember.

  • Hebrew always sets right to left. Set dir="rtl" in HTML and use the right-to-left paragraph setting in Word or InDesign — never fake it with spaces or by reversing characters.
  • זצ״ל after Harav Eliezer Geldzahler's name and שליט״א after Harav Yosef Halpert's name are never dropped, abbreviated differently, or moved.
  • Use the Hebrew punctuation marks — gershayim ״ (U+05F4) and geresh ׳ (U+05F3) — not the English double and single quote. Most keyboards give you the wrong one by default.
  • Spell the names as they appear in the crest: געלדצעהלער and האלפערט. Don't re-transliterate them.
  • When both scripts appear on one page, give each its own block. Don't run Hebrew and English on the same line unless it's a single word.

Never retype the Hebrew inside the crest artwork. If a line needs to change — a new nasi, a corrected spelling — the artwork gets rebuilt from the vector file by a designer, not patched over in Word.

Flyers, invitations, and programs

  • Crest centered at the top, 2.5–3.5 in wide, with the gold double rule beneath it.
  • Hebrew headline in Frank Ruhl Libre Black, set right to left, on its own line.
  • Ivory or parchment background rather than plain white — it makes the gold sit correctly.
  • One ornament per page. The crest is the ornament; don't add scroll borders or corner flourishes around it.

Signage, plaques, and donor walls

  • Exterior and interior signs: cartouche or full crest in brushed or polished brass on a dark ground, with the Hebrew name cut in Frank Ruhl Libre Black.
  • Dedication plaques: one-color bronze version, etched or cast. English dedication lines in Cinzel, Hebrew in Frank Ruhl Libre.
  • Minimum viewing distance rule of thumb: 1 in of Hebrew letter height for every 10 ft of reading distance.

Production notes

Get the vector file before printing anything large. The artwork this package was built from is 400 × 241 pixels. At true print resolution that is only about 1⅓ inches wide. The larger files here were interpolated up, which is fine on screen and acceptable on a small letterhead crest, but it will look soft on a banner, a sign, a full-page program cover, or anything foil stamped.

Ask whoever created the crest for the .ai, .eps, .pdf, or .svg original. If that no longer exists, have a designer redraw the crest as vector once — it is a few hours of work and it never has to be done again.

Sending files to a printer

  • Send vector if you have it. If not, send the largest PNG and state the final printed size so they can flag it.
  • Convert to CMYK using the values in the color section — don't let the printer's software guess.
  • Ask for a printed proof on the actual stock for anything foil stamped or on colored paper.

Working files

  • Keep this package in one folder and hand the whole folder over, not a screenshot of the logo pulled off a flyer.
  • Never re-export a logo from a Word document or a WhatsApp image. Both recompress it.
  • When the identity changes, update the version number on the cover of this document.

Quick checklist before anything goes out

  • Correct variant for the background it's sitting on.
  • Scaled proportionally, above minimum size, level, no effects added.
  • Clear space respected on all four sides.
  • Hebrew reads right to left and both honorifics are intact.
  • Address matches: 1146 N Maple Ave, Toms River NJ 08755.
